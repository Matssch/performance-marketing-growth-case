# Customer Segmentation

## Why segmentation mattered

One of the key lessons at MoveMove was that not every customer should move through the same buying journey. A small entrepreneur looking for a few mobility cards behaves differently from a larger company managing dozens of employees, vehicles and internal stakeholders.
The product may be similar, but the buying process is not. That meant the growth model needed to reflect differences in:

- Customer size
- Commercial value
- Buying complexity
- Need for Sales support
- Decision-making process
- Conversion journey

Rather than forcing every prospect through one standard funnel, we worked with different go-to-market motions based on customer size.

## Segmentation model

The model was structured around three broad customer groups.

### 1 – 25 | Self-service

Smaller customers were primarily suited to a digital self-service journey. The objective was to make acquisition and conversion as simple and scalable as possible. The journey looked roughly like:

**Demand → Website → Product information → Conversion → Onboarding**

Marketing therefore had a strong role across:

- Paid Search
- Paid Social
- Affiliate marketing
- SEO
- Landing pages
- CRO
- CRM
- Onboarding

The key commercial question was:

**Can we acquire and convert this customer efficiently without creating unnecessary Sales involvement?**

For this segment, the economics depend heavily on digital acquisition efficiency.

## 21 – 50 | Hybrid journey

As the customer became larger, the buying process became more complex. There could be more questions, more users, more operational requirements and a greater need for human interaction. The journey became more hybrid:

**Marketing → Website → Qualification → Sales interaction → Customer**

Marketing still played an important role in generating and converting demand, but Sales could become part of the journey when the opportunity required it.

This meant success could no longer be measured only through direct online conversion.

Lead quality and progression became more important.

## 50 + | Sales-led

Larger accounts were increasingly suited to a Sales-led journey. The potential commercial value was higher, but so was the complexity of the buying process. The journey looked more like:

**Demand → Account engagement → Lead / enquiry → Sales → Commercial process → Customer**

Marketing's role changed. Instead of only driving direct online conversion, Marketing needed to help:

- Create demand
- Generate relevant account engagement
- Provide strong customer proof
- Support Sales
- Build trust
- Create qualified opportunities

The larger the account, the less useful it became to judge Marketing only on direct website conversion.

## Why this changed the funnel

Without segmentation, it would be easy to optimise everything toward one outcome:

**Online conversion**

But that would create the wrong behaviour for larger opportunities.

+ For a small customer, direct conversion could be exactly the right outcome.
+ For a larger account, a Sales conversation could be much more valuable.

The funnel therefore needed to reflect the customer.

#### 1 – 20
Demand → Website → Self-service conversion → Customer

#### 21 – 50
Demand → Website → Qualification → Sales / self-service → Customer


#### 50+
Demand → Account engagement → Qualified opportunity → Sales process → Customer
