# Segment Strategy

## Why the strategy changes by segment

Customer size does not only change the buying journey.

It also changes:

- What message is most relevant
- Which channels make sense
- Which CTA should be used
- How much Sales should be involved
- Which KPI matters most

The mistake would be to run one broad campaign and expect every customer group to behave in the same way.

The better approach is to adapt the growth motion to the economics and complexity of each segment.

---

## Segment 1 — 1–20 customers

### Commercial role

This is the most scalable segment.

The customer value is lower, but the buying journey can be much more digital and self-service.

The goal is therefore:

**Acquire efficiently and convert with as little friction as possible.**

### Main message

Focus on:

- Simplicity
- Convenience
- Speed
- Ease of use
- Less administration
- Clear value without a complex Sales process

### Main channels

- Paid Search
- Paid Social
- Affiliates
- Organic search
- Retargeting
- CRM

### Main CTA

**Start now**

or another low-friction self-service action.

### Sales involvement

Limited.

Sales should only become involved when there is a clear reason.

### Main KPIs

- CPA
- CAC
- Conversion rate
- Customer volume
- Cost per visit
- Funnel completion
- Onboarding completion

### Growth focus

The main growth levers are:

- Better traffic quality
- Lower acquisition cost
- Stronger landing pages
- Better mobile experience
- Simpler conversion
- Better onboarding

This segment is mainly about **scale and efficiency**.

---

## Segment 2 — 21–50 customers

### Commercial role

This segment sits between self-service and Sales-led acquisition.

The potential value is higher, but the buying journey is also more complex.

The goal becomes:

**Keep the digital journey efficient while creating the option for Sales support when needed.**

### Main message

Focus on:

- Efficiency
- Control
- Ease of management
- Administrative savings
- Scalability
- Support when required

### Main channels

- Paid Search
- Paid Social
- SEO
- Retargeting
- CRM
- Customer cases
- Sales follow-up

### Main CTA

Depending on buying intent:

**Start now**

or

**Talk to us**

This is where CTA strategy becomes more important because forcing everyone into one path can reduce conversion.

### Sales involvement

Selective.

Sales should become involved when:

- Customer value is high enough
- Buying complexity increases
- The prospect requests support
- There is a clear commercial opportunity

### Main KPIs

- Qualified leads
- Lead-to-customer conversion
- Customer acquisition cost
- Sales-assisted conversion
- Average customer value
- Funnel progression

### Growth focus

The main growth levers are:

- Better qualification
- Clearer routing
- Segment-specific messaging
- Stronger customer proof
- Faster follow-up
- Better digital-to-Sales handoff

This segment is mainly about **balancing efficiency with commercial support**.

---

## Segment 3 — 50+ customers

### Commercial role

This is the highest-value segment, but also the least suitable for a pure self-service model.

The buying process may involve multiple stakeholders, more questions and a stronger need for trust.

The goal becomes:

**Create and identify high-value demand, then help Sales convert it.**

### Main message

Focus on:

- Business value
- Operational efficiency
- Control
- Reporting
- Scalability
- Customer support
- Reliability

### Main channels

- Paid Social
- Targeted campaigns
- Search
- SEO
- Customer cases
- Partnerships
- Content
- Sales outreach
- Events where relevant

### Main CTA

**Talk to Sales**

or another high-intent commercial action.

### Sales involvement

High.

Marketing's role is not to replace Sales.

It is to:

- Create demand
- Identify relevant accounts
- Build trust
- Provide proof
- Support Sales conversations
- Improve opportunity quality

### Main KPIs

- Qualified opportunities
- Pipeline
- Cost per opportunity
- Sales conversion
- Customer value
- Sales cycle
- Win rate

### Growth focus

The main growth levers are:

- Account prioritisation
- Stronger customer proof
- Better Sales enablement
- More targeted campaigns
- Buying-group engagement
- Higher-quality opportunities

This segment is mainly about **quality and commercial value**.

## Segment comparison

| Area | 1–20 | 21–50 | 50+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary motion | Self-service | Hybrid | Sales-led |
| Main objective | Scale | Efficient qualification | High-value opportunities |
| Sales involvement | Low | Medium | High |
| Main CTA | Start now | Start / Talk to us | Talk to Sales |
| Main KPI | CPA / CAC | Qualified conversion | Pipeline |
| Key lever | CRO | Routing + proof | Account targeting |
| Buying complexity | Low | Medium | High |

---

## Why this matters for performance marketing

The same channel can perform differently depending on the segment.

For example, Paid Search may work very well for self-service demand capture.

Paid Social may be more valuable when reaching larger business audiences or supporting demand creation.

A higher CPA can still be acceptable if:

- Customer value is higher
- Opportunity quality is stronger
- Sales conversion is better
- Retention is stronger

That is why segment economics need to sit behind channel optimisation.

The question is not only:

**Which channel has the lowest CPA?**

The better question is:

**Which combination of segment, channel and journey creates the strongest commercial return?**

---

## Core principle

**Different customer economics require different growth motions.**

The best funnel is not the one that looks simplest in a dashboard.

It is the one that matches how the customer actually buys.
