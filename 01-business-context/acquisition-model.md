# Acquisition Model

## How the acquisition system worked

MoveMove had a largely digital customer acquisition model.

Paid media played an important role in generating demand, but acquisition performance was not determined by media alone.

The full system looked more like this:

**Demand → Traffic → Website → Conversion → Onboarding → Customer**

Different channels entered that system at different points and had different roles.

## Acquisition channels

The main acquisition mix included:

- Paid Search
- Paid Social
- Affiliate marketing
- Organic traffic
- Direct traffic
- Brand and offline activity

Paid Search was the largest digital acquisition channel and captured a significant amount of existing demand.

Paid Social was added to broaden the acquisition mix and create demand outside Search.

Affiliate marketing provided an additional performance-based source of customers.

Organic, direct and brand traffic also contributed to the overall website journey.

## The acquisition model

I looked at the system in four layers.

### 1. Demand

The first question was whether we were reaching the right potential customers.

Demand could already exist, for example through people actively searching for a mobility solution, or it could be created through brand and paid social activity.

This distinction mattered because not every channel should be judged in exactly the same way.

### 2. Traffic

The next layer was traffic quality.

I looked beyond traffic volume and considered:

- Channel
- Campaign
- Audience
- Search intent
- Cost per click
- Landing page
- Device
- Conversion behaviour

More traffic only created value if the traffic had a realistic chance of becoming a customer.

### 3. Conversion

Once traffic reached the website, media performance and website performance became connected.

This is where CRO became an important part of the acquisition model.

The main questions became:

- Are visitors landing on the right page?
- Is the proposition clear?
- Is the next step obvious?
- Where do people drop out?
- Is there unnecessary friction?
- Does the journey match the type of customer?

Improving conversion meant we could generate more customers from traffic we were already paying for.

### 4. Customer

The conversion itself was not the final step.

The onboarding journey also mattered.

After someone converted, the experience needed to move them successfully toward becoming an active customer.

That meant acquisition, website optimisation and CRM could not be managed as completely separate activities.

## Paid acquisition economics

For paid acquisition, I used CPA as one of the key commercial guardrails.

At the starting point, CPA was approximately **€100**.

The target was approximately **€62.50**, with around **€70** as the maximum level we wanted to operate around sustainably.

This meant growth could not come from simply increasing media spend.

The acquisition model needed to improve through a combination of:

**Better traffic quality + stronger conversion + better customer journey**

## Website performance

At the start of the optimisation period:

- Website traffic was approximately **40,000 visits per month**
- Website conversion rate was approximately **1.2%**

These metrics describe overall website performance and should not be treated as the exact denominator behind paid-media CPA.

That distinction is important.

Website conversion tells us how efficiently total website demand was being converted.

CPA tells us how efficiently paid acquisition investment was producing customers.

Together, they help diagnose the wider acquisition system, but they answer different questions.

## Channel roles

### Paid Search — Demand capture

Paid Search primarily captured existing intent.

Someone searching for a relevant mobility solution was already relatively close to the category.

The focus was therefore on:

- Search intent
- CPC
- Conversion
- CPA
- Landing-page relevance
- Scale

### Paid Social — Demand creation and expansion

Paid Social had a different role.

It allowed us to reach relevant audiences before they were necessarily searching for the product.

The objective was not to copy Search and expect identical performance.

It created another route into the acquisition funnel and reduced reliance on one major paid channel.

### Affiliates — Performance-based acquisition

Affiliate marketing added another acquisition source where performance could be evaluated against customer volume and acquisition cost.

### Organic and Direct — Existing and created demand

Organic and direct traffic helped show demand that was not directly purchased through paid media.

Growth in these areas could also indicate stronger brand awareness, SEO performance or repeat consideration.
