# Paid Search

## Role in the acquisition model

Paid Search was the largest digital acquisition channel for the mobility company, with approximately **€30K in monthly spend**.

Its main role was demand capture.

People using Search were already showing intent around mobility products and related business needs, which made the channel highly relevant for direct customer acquisition.

The objective was not simply to generate more clicks.

It was to capture enough qualified demand at an acquisition cost that allowed the business to keep growing sustainably.

---

## The commercial objective

The main performance guardrail was CPA.

At the starting point, CPA was approximately **€100**.

The target was approximately **€62.50**, with around **€70** as the maximum level we wanted to operate around sustainably.

That meant Paid Search had two jobs:

1. Maintain enough acquisition volume
2. Improve efficiency without damaging customer quality

This created a constant balance between **scale and efficiency**.

Reducing spend can improve CPA very quickly.

It can also stop growth.

The goal was therefore not to create the lowest possible CPA.

The goal was to find the strongest sustainable combination of:

**Volume × Conversion × Acquisition Cost**

---

## Search intent

I would not treat every keyword as having the same commercial value.

The first distinction is intent.

### High intent

Searches where the user is actively looking for a product or solution.

Examples:

- Business mobility card
- Fuel card for company
- Mobility solution for employees
- Business fuel card

These searches are relatively close to conversion.

The main focus is:

- Impression share
- CPC
- Conversion rate
- CPA
- Landing-page relevance

### Comparison intent

The buyer understands the category but is still evaluating alternatives.

Examples:

- Best business fuel card
- Mobility card comparison
- Fuel card costs
- Business mobility providers

These searches can be commercially valuable but often require more information before conversion.

The landing page therefore needs stronger:

- Product comparison
- Benefits
- Pricing clarity
- Customer proof
- FAQs

### Problem intent

The buyer has a relevant problem but may not yet know the product category.

Examples:

- Reduce business travel administration
- Manage employee fuel expenses
- Business mileage administration

These keywords can create additional demand but normally sit further away from direct conversion.

I would therefore evaluate them differently from high-intent search.

## Campaign structure

I prefer campaign structure to follow commercial intent rather than creating unnecessary complexity.

A simplified structure could look like:

```text
Paid Search
│
├── Brand
│
├── High-intent product
│
├── Problem / category
│
├── Competitor / comparison
