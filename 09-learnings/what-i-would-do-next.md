# What I Would Do Next

## Why this section matters

The original growth work was focused on improving acquisition efficiency, website conversion and the customer journey. If I were building the same growth system today, I would keep the same commercial foundation but take the measurement and optimisation model further. The biggest shift would be moving from:
**How efficiently are we acquiring customers?** toward: **Which customers create the strongest long-term commercial value, and where should we invest to acquire more of them?**

---

## 1. Move from CPA toward full CAC

CPA was an important performance guardrail, but CPA only shows part of the acquisition economics. I would build a fuller CAC model including, where relevant:

- Paid media
- Agency costs
- Creative production
- Marketing technology
- Sales cost
- Affiliate fees
- Other acquisition-related costs

The purpose would not be to make the metric more complicated. It would be to understand the real cost of growth.

---

## 2. Connect acquisition to customer value

The next step would be to connect acquisition source and customer segment to downstream value. I would want to understand:

- Customer value by channel
- Customer value by segment
- Activation by acquisition source
- Retention by acquisition source
- Expansion
- Referral
- Payback

This would help prevent a common performance marketing mistake: **Optimising toward the cheapest customer instead of the best customer.**

---

## 3. Build cohort analysis

Blended averages can hide changes in customer quality. I would therefore analyse customers in cohorts based on:

- Acquisition month
- Channel
- Campaign
- Segment
- Proposition

Then compare:

- Activation
- Retention
- Customer value
- Payback
- Expansion
